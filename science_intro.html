Foundations of Science

Etymology, Definition & Fundamental Pillars

ETYMOLOGY

Origin of the Word

The term "Science" originates from the Latin root word "Scientia", which literally translates to Knowledge.

Scientia (Latin) → "To Know" / Structured Knowledge
DEFINITION

What is Science?

Science is the systematic study of the structure and behaviour of nature and the physical world through observation, experimentation, and evidence.

Any systematic knowledge concerned with the physical world and its phenomena.
PILLARS

Pioneering Pillars

Father of Science: Aristotle
Father of Modern Science: Albert Einstein / Galileo
Mother of Science: Philosophy
Mother of Modern Science: Maria Skłodowska-Curie (Polish Scientist)

Physics

The King of Science • Matter, Energy & Forces

KING OF SCIENCE (Maths: Queen of Science)

Study of Matter & Energy

Etymology: Derived from the Greek word "Physika" (or Physis), meaning Nature.

Definition: The branch of science in which we systematically study matter, energy, motion, and their mutual interactions.

Founding Pioneer
Father of Physics: Galileo Galilei
Key Contributors: Isaac Newton & Albert Einstein
1. Mechanics

Study of forces, motion, friction, gravitation, and mechanical equilibrium.

2. Thermodynamics

Study of heat transfer, temperature, entropy, and thermal mechanical work.

3. Electromagnetism

Study of electric charges, magnetic fields, induction, and electromagnetic radiation.

4. Optics

Study of light behaviour, reflection, refraction, lenses, and lasers.

5. Nuclear Physics

Study of atomic nuclei, their internal structure, nuclear fission, fusion, and radiation.

Chemistry

The Central Science • Atoms, Molecules & Reactions

CENTRAL SCIENCE Bridges Physics & Life

Composition & Transformation

Etymology: Derived from the Greek word "Chemeia" (or ancient Egyptian Khemia), meaning "Cast together" or "Pour together".

Definition: The branch of science that studies atoms, molecules, and ions, and how they combine to create substances and compounds.

Pioneer of Chemical Laws
Father of Chemistry: Antoine Lavoisier
Modern Chemistry: Antoine Lavoisier, Robert Boyle & Berzelius
1. Organic Chemistry

Study of carbon-containing compounds and covalent biological structures.

2. Inorganic Chemistry

Study of minerals, metals, salts, oxides, and non-carbon compounds.

3. Physical Chemistry

Application of physics, thermodynamics, and kinetics to chemical reactions.

4. Analytical Chemistry

Instruments and techniques for separation, identification, and quantitative analysis.

5. Biochemistry

Chemical processes occurring inside living cells (enzymes, DNA, metabolism).

Biology

The Science of Life • Structure, Growth & Ecosystems

STUDY OF LIFE Greek: Bios + Logos

Living Organisms & Functions

Etymology: Derived from two Greek words: "Bios" (Life) + "Logos" (Study or Discourse).

Definition: The branch of natural science that studies living organisms, cellular morphology, physiology, and evolutionary genetics.

Pioneers of Biology
Father of Biology: Aristotle
Term Coined By: Jean-Baptiste Lamarck & Gottfried Treviranus (1802)
1. Zoology (Animals) Greek: Zōion + Logos

The scientific study of animals, their behaviour, structure, and classification.

Father of Zoology: Aristotle
2. Botany (Plants) Greek: Botane

The study of plants, flora, photosynthesis, plant physiology, and taxonomy.

Father of Botany: Theophrastus
Other Vital Biological Branches
Microbiology

Microscopic organisms (bacteria, viruses, fungi).

Father: Leeuwenhoek
Genetics

Heredity, DNA, and inherited variation.

Father: Gregor Mendel
Cytology

Structure and function of microscopic cells.

Father: Robert Hooke
Ecology

Interactions between organisms & habitats.
